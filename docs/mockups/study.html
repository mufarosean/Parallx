Study

Every screen of the Study extension, built from parts the app already has, in the app's own tokens. The Practice, Test and Source frames are live: click an option, type an answer, press Next. Nothing here is a new visual language; the two new parts are the progress strand and the anchor quote, and both are drawn from existing tokens.

Second pass, 2026-10-07: the owner found the first pass busy, and it was. This pass removes a second row of actions under every card, the key-hint chips, the position counter beside the strand, the page and format from the eyebrow, the hint sentences on the setup sheet, the model row (now a footer line), the number tiles on the generation screen, and two sidebar sections. What is left is what each screen is for.

The question content is illustrative, written the way real generated questions will read: one concept each, an anchor on the page, competitive wrong options.

0. Principles and shared parts

  • One thing on screen. A session shows one question, its answer area, and nothing else until the answer is in. Feedback appears under the card, never over it. Toolbar, strand and the scope line are the only chrome.
  • The card is the study stock. The question sits on the Flashcards white serif card, so a card in Study and a card in Flashcards are visibly the same material. Everything around it is the app's dark chrome.
  • The anchor is always one glance away. Every question carries its page and quote. After an answer the quote is shown; Show Source opens the PDF beside the session at that page with the quote highlighted. A question with no anchor does not exist.
  • The strand is the progress. Twenty thin segments in the toolbar: faint for unanswered, green for right, red for wrong, accent for the current one. No percentages, no score counter during the session. The score is the strand.
  • One primary action, and quiet everything else. Next is the one filled button on a session screen. Show Source, Explain and Retry are text on the verdict line. Skip is a ghost. A screen never shows two button clusters.
  • Nothing is said twice. The strand is the count, so there is no "7 of 20". The anchor carries the page, so the eyebrow does not. The card's shape says the format, so no format badge. Keys are a faint glyph on the control that takes them, not a row of chips.
  • Disclosure by frequency. What changes every session (scope, mode, answer) is on the sheet. What changes rarely (model, context) is a line in its footer. What explains a control is its tooltip, not a sentence beside it.
  • Motion is for state, not decoration. Each transition says what changed: a card leaves left and the next arrives from below; feedback rises under the card; rubric points land one after another; the source pane widens from the right. Durations are the motion tokens (120, 180, 260 ms) and nothing else. Reduced motion collapses them all.
  • The sidebar is the desk. Study has its own activity-bar container: every material with its coverage, the sessions open now, the weak concepts across all of them. Several sessions run at once, one per tab, and several materials can be studied together in one session.
  • The model is a choice on the sheet. Which local model writes and grades, and how large a window it gets, is picked where the session starts and shown where it runs. Never hidden in Settings alone.
  • Keys for everything. 1 to 4 pick an option, Enter checks or advances, S opens the source, R retries, Esc closes the source. Shown faintly in the controls row, never as a help screen.

3. The Cape Cod Method

The LDF method requires one parameter for each accident year, the ultimate loss ULTi, in addition to the two parameters of the growth curve. The Cape Cod method instead assumes that the expected ultimate loss for each year is a constant expected loss ratio applied to the year's on-level premium, so that the whole triangle is fit with only three parameters: ω, θ and ELR.

μi,t = Premiumi · ELR · [G(xt) − G(xt−1)]

Fewer parameters means more degrees of freedom in the data and, in general, a smaller parameter variance. The cost is the assumption that the loss ratio is stable across accident years after adjusting premium to a common level.

Pick an option. Right: the row and key turn green, the strand segment fills. Wrong: the row turns red, the correct row turns green, and the reason and the anchor rise under the card.
  • The card is the Flashcards stock: white, serif, 20 px bold question. Options are rows on the card with a keycap at the left, divided by the card's own hairline. No radio circles, no letters A to D: the keycap is the letter and the key.
  • The strand segment for the current question is accent; on answer it turns green or red and briefly thickens (spring, 180 ms) so the eye catches the result without a toast. It is the only progress indicator: no counter, no percentage.
  • Why is one or two sentences grounded in the anchor, written at generation time, never a live free answer. Explain is the live door: it opens chat with the question, the anchor and the page attached.
  • Retry only after a wrong answer, once: the wrong row stays marked, the others clear. A retried right answer counts as wrong for mastery and the strand keeps its red.
  • Skip puts the question at the end of the session; the strand shows it faint until it comes round.

Mack's model rests on three assumptions: (i) E[Ci,k+1 | Ci,1, …, Ci,k] = Ci,k fk; (ii) the accident years {Ci,1, …, Ci,I} are independent; (iii) Var(Ci,k+1 | Ci,1, …, Ci,k) = Ci,k σk².

What is new to the core

  1. The editor/title menu location, so a tool can add an entry to a pane's More Actions with a when on the active editor. First user: Study.
  2. The question provider seam, so a tool can offer questions (text, answer, rubric, origin, where to open) to any other. First providers: Worksheets' essay and qualitative problems, Flashcards' essay-practice cards.
  3. Nothing else. The strand, the anchor quote, the coverage bar and the card options are Study's own CSS on existing tokens.
